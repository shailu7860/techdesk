# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Three buyer types, weighted equally; the page must work for all of them without splitting into audience-specific paths:

- **Founders / startup leaders** who need an MVP, SaaS platform or AI product built. Often non-technical. They judge speed, craft and whether TechDesk "gets" products.
- **Business owners (India-first, SMB)** who want automation, web presence, marketing and internal tools. They judge trust and plainness, and prefer WhatsApp or a phone call over forms.
- **Enterprise / CTO buyers** who need systems, integrations and AI agents. They judge engineering depth, architecture thinking and reliability.

Context: first visit is usually from a referral, LinkedIn or search, often on mobile. The job is to decide within a minute whether TechDesk is serious enough to contact, then to contact them with as little friction as possible (WhatsApp, call, quote calculator or multi-step brief).

## Product Purpose

The marketing website for **TechDesk**, a technology studio building AI agents, software platforms, automation, web products and digital marketing. It exists to turn qualified visitors into conversations.

It is also TechDesk's strongest portfolio piece: the build quality of the site is evidence of the build quality of the work.

Success looks like:
- Visitors understand what TechDesk does, in business outcomes, within the first screen.
- Real projects (Biexor, Stratos, BidMaster, 1Bull and client builds) read as engineered systems, not screenshots.
- Qualified leads arrive through WhatsApp, calls, the quote calculator and the contact brief.

## Brand Personality

**Precise, calm-confident, inventive, with warmth.**

- **Precise**: engineered, exact, nothing decorative without a job.
- **Calm-confident**: quiet authority. It never shouts, never over-claims, never uses hype words.
- **Inventive**: moments a visitor has not seen before, the "how was this built?" reaction.
- **Warm**: a human is one tap away. Plain language, a real WhatsApp line, not a faceless enterprise vendor.

In the first 30 seconds a visitor should feel, in this order: **awe** (the site proves capability), **clarity** (I understand what they do), **trust** (they are serious), **momentum** (starting is easy).

Voice: short declarative sentences, business outcomes before technology names, no superlatives, no invented numbers.

References:
- **Active Theory / Lusion**: cinematic WebGL moments and immersive storytelling. Borrow the *awe* for one or two signature moments, not the whole page.
- **Stripe**: clarity alongside delightful detail; product visuals that explain rather than decorate; excellent information hierarchy.

## Anti-references

- Generic agency templates: hero plus three icon cards plus testimonials plus a pricing grid. The old portfolio site and `digital-ascent` are exactly this; do not resemble them.
- A fake spaceship dashboard: HUD chrome everywhere, blinking panels, gaming aesthetics, Orbitron-style "sci-fi" fonts.
- Neon overload: multiple glowing accent colours, gradient text, cheap particle backgrounds, random glows.
- Generic AI imagery: glowing brains, robot hands, blue circuit boards.
- The SaaS hero-metric template: big number, small label, gradient accent. Especially with invented stats.
- Liquid-glass or glassmorphism used as decoration.
- Motion that makes you wait: long preloaders, scroll-jacking that fights input, animation used as filler.

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the proof.** Every signature moment demonstrates a capability TechDesk sells (AI workflow, system architecture, performance). If an effect proves nothing, cut it.
2. **Outcome before technology.** Lead with what the client gets; the stack is supporting evidence, not the headline.
3. **Cinema with a spine.** Content, then layout, then type, then interaction, then motion, then 3D. Awe is earned on top of a page that already works statically, with reduced motion, and on a mid-range phone.
4. **A human is one tap away.** WhatsApp, call, quote and chat are always reachable, never buried. Momentum beats persuasion.
5. **Never over-claim.** Real projects, real details, honest placeholders. Credibility is the product.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.2 AA** across the site: 4.5:1 body contrast (3:1 large text and UI), full keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic landmarks, labelled form fields, alt text written in the brand voice.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: cinematic transitions, parallax, scroll-pinning and WebGL are replaced with instant or crossfade equivalents. Content never depends on an animation firing to become visible.
- Colour is never the only carrier of state (system status, form errors, calculator selections).
- Mobile and low-end devices get a simplified, fast experience rather than a shrunken desktop one. Touch targets are at least 44px.
- Plain-English copy suited to readers whose first language is not English.
